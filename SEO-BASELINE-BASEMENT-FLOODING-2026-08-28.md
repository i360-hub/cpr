### Three findings the handoff brief did not name

1. **"Who to call" is the single largest intent on the page.** Five near-identical
   variants — *who to call when basement floods / basement flooding who to call /
   who to call for basement flooding / who to call for flooded basement / who to
   call when your basement floods* — total **570 impressions**, more than double
   the top single query. All sit at position 48–60. The page does not answer that
   question directly anywhere. This is the highest-leverage gap.

2. **Locally qualified intent already ranks far better than generic intent.**
   `basement flooding cleanup charlotte` is at **13**, `flooded basement near me`
   at **18**, `basement flood cleanup charlotte` at **28**, `basement flooding
   cleanup near me` at **31** — while every generic national-intent query sits at
   50–74. Clicks, when they come, will come from the qualified set. The expansion
   should lean into metro qualification rather than chase the generic head terms.

3. **Part of the impression volume is unservable geography.** New Bern, Morehead
   City, Asheville, and Beaufort SC queries are all well outside the service area.
   They inflate the impression count and can never convert. Judge the post-change
   result on the qualified subset, not on raw impressions.

## How to read the delta

Re-run the same GSC query for 28 days after the change lands and compare:

- **Primary success signal:** first organic clicks against a 0-click baseline.
- **Secondary:** movement on the five "who to call" variants from ~48–60.
- **Tertiary:** `basement flooding cleanup charlotte` (13) and `flooded basement
  near me` (18) reaching page one.
- **Ignore:** raw impression totals, which are diluted by out-of-area queries.

Do **not** read anything into this before ~4 weeks post-change.
